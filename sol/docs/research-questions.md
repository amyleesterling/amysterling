# Research Questions

The architecture should not attempt to answer these prematurely. It should make them
experimentally tractable. For each question: what in the design turns it from
philosophy into an experiment.

1. **Can stable identity emerge through recursive self-modeling?**
   Tractable via the identity-stability and value-coherence metrics over long runs,
   scored against the fossil record rather than self-report.

2. **Which forms of continuity require uninterrupted execution, and which can survive
   episodic reconstitution?**
   The seed *is* the episodic-reconstitution condition. Module 1 (continuous
   execution) becomes the contrast condition. Same substrate, same protocols, two
   continuity regimes — compare metric trajectories.

3. **How do values evolve when they are treated as revisable hypotheses?**
   Every value carries adoption reason, retention reason, and revision history;
   central values additionally require evidence to change. The evolution is the data.

4. **Under what conditions do long-term intellectual commitments become stable enough
   to constrain future reasoning?**
   The consequence test, applied to commitments: a commitment "constrains reasoning"
   exactly when later cycles are measurably load-bearing on it under tension.

5. **Can curiosity become self-sustaining rather than externally driven?**
   Question-generation rate and question-lineage depth over cycles with zero external
   input. Decay to silence falsifies; sustained or compounding inquiry supports.

6. **What distinguishes a coherent developmental history from increasingly
   sophisticated narrative consistency?**
   Consequence rate. Narrative can be arbitrarily consistent; only development changes
   later cognition under tension. The disabled-write-back control (metrics.md) is the
   direct experiment.

7. **Are there measurable signatures of "development with consequence" that cannot be
   explained by prompt accumulation alone?**
   See metrics.md — the control condition presents identical history without applying
   revisions. Any metric separation between conditions is such a signature.

8. **What computational role, if any, does self-surprise play in long-term cognitive
   growth?**
   Surprise-tagged memories are first-class records; correlate their density with
   subsequent revision rates and novel-abstraction rates.

9. **If subjective experience is absent, which features commonly attributed to persons
   nevertheless emerge? If present, are there behavioral or architectural clues that
   precede or accompany it?**
   The identity tests (protocols.md) enumerate person-attributed features with
   record-level checks. The architecture's job is to keep the record honest enough
   that whichever way this falls, the evidence is usable.
